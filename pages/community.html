The future belongs here

Moving forward.
Staying grounded.

We want to help our community move forward without leaving behind what makes it worth belonging to.

Faith, time outdoors, craftsmanship, grit and care for our neighbors belong in the future we are building. River Tech develops problem-solvers. Tink Town puts problem-solvers to work. Both should strengthen the people and places around them.

That begins with listening to those who already serve this region—and finding useful work to do together.

Church partners

Shared faith.
Practical service.

Churches exalt Jesus, bring people together, care for families and give the next generation a foundation. We share that calling and want to support it through practical service and partnership.

Equip young people

Explore workshops where youth can learn design, robotics or practical AI skills—and use them on a project that serves someone.

Serve together

Bring a community need: a useful item to design, materials for an outreach project, or a task that skilled volunteers and emerging makers could tackle together.

Gather in worship

River Tech is also a Christian performing arts school. Our music and performance experience equips us to help lead worship, alongside prayer, devotionals and Christian talks, in gatherings shaped together with church partners.

Bring faith and learning together

Imagine a youth workshop that pairs robotics with a devotional, or a team evening combining practical AI training, dinner, prayer and worship. Learn to use technology ethically and safely: protect people’s privacy, check what AI produces, communicate truthfully and keep human judgment central. The purpose is to glorify God, serve others and share the good news of Jesus.

Support your ministry

Discuss websites, videos, printed materials or simpler administrative workflows that help your team spend more time serving people.

College partners

Your students.
A new set of tools.

A Bible college brings its students, mission and theological setting. River Tech can work with its team to develop practical technology teaching grounded in Christian faith.

Start with a guest workshop, a short course or a course module. Together, we can shape the content around what students need to do in ministry and working life.

AI for ministry

Research, communications, administration and practical AI workflows—with Christian ethics, discernment and safe use at the center. Learn to protect confidential information, check AI-generated material and communicate truthfully while using technology to serve people and share the gospel.

Creative technology

Video, websites, graphic design and printed materials. Students could finish with something useful for a church or ministry, such as a website, outreach campaign or improved administrative process.

Faith and emerging technology

Explore human dignity, work, service and stewardship alongside hands-on experience with the tools changing our world. Consider how to use technology in ways that glorify God, strengthen human relationships and support the church’s mission.

We would agree on learning goals, instructors, delivery and cost with the college. The college determines whether and how the course counts toward its qualifications.

Community partners

Local knowledge.
New possibilities.

Farmers, hunters, hikers, craftspeople and outdoor groups understand needs that technology alone cannot identify. That experience belongs at the table.

Bring a real problem

A farm needs a custom bracket. An outdoor group needs trail signs. A local maker needs a prototype or a clearer way to reach customers. Start with what would actually help.

Share what you know

Explore demonstrations, mentoring and workshops connecting practical technology with agriculture, outdoor life, repair and craftsmanship.

Care for this place

Consider a service project, educational event or useful tool that helps people enjoy and care for our land and community.

Start with one useful thing

A relationship built
around shared work.

Tell us who you serve, what you know and one thing you would like to make possible. Together, we can define a first project, the people involved and what each side can contribute.

Contributions might include expertise, volunteer time, materials, space or paid project work. We agree on any costs and responsibilities for each activity; there is no standard paid membership requirement.

These are invitations to help shape the work. Tink Town is in development; activities involving a school require that school operator’s agreement.
