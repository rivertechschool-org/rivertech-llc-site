01 / Provide the foundation

Provide a home.

Help provide a home for a River Tech school, Tink Town, or both. We are looking for practical space in North Idaho that can be approved for the activities it will actually host.

How this could work

A zoning designation and an occupancy approval answer different questions. Planning must allow the use; building and fire officials must approve the building for it. An office, retail unit or church building is not automatically ready for a school or a café and workshop.

A practical starting point

The space to make it work.

Three ways to give the vision a home. The Tink Town and combined-site figures describe the fuller facility—not the space needed to launch. These working estimates include indoor support space and circulation; parking and outdoor recreation space are additional.

Combined location — how sharing saves space

The two illustrative plans total 14,000 sq ft. Sharing about 1,500 sq ft through a kitchen, lunch / performance room, conference space and selected support areas gives a compact example of 12,500 sq ft. Around 14,000 sq ft is our starting search target, with more room where the layout or growth plans call for it.

Shared rooms need compatible schedules. The school needs controlled access, and fabrication must be separated from students, customers and food service.

These are planning estimates, not approved building plans or legal occupancy limits. The 1,500 sq ft production allowance must be checked against selected equipment, safe working clearances and material flow. Final capacity depends on the property layout and required approvals.

For a River Tech school

Our planning target is an average of about 15 students per teaching classroom. Some rooms may accommodate 12 if larger rooms keep the school-wide classroom average near 15. This is our operating requirement, not a legal occupancy formula.

  • School use must be permitted by the zoning district or approved through the required land-use process. There is not one universal “school zoning” label.
  • Classroom layouts must fit students, a teacher, furniture and clear circulation. The building official determines permitted occupant loads; room dimensions and exits matter.
  • Confirm educational occupancy approval, including any change-of-use permit and certificate of occupancy required for the premises.
  • Check compliant exits and travel paths, emergency lighting and signs, alarms, extinguishers, fire separations, and sprinklers where required.
  • Check accessible entry and circulation, suitable toilets, ventilation, lighting, and safe arrival and pickup arrangements.

For Tink Town

Tink Town can begin without a dedicated venue, then grow into a small public hub and eventually a fuller café and workshop. The requirements below describe that fuller facility, not a day-one lease. See the three-phase launch plan on the Tink Town page.

  • Front of house: café counter and seating, a place to discuss projects and display finished work, and flexible space for workshops and events.
  • Work areas: desks for design, AI and marketing work; a clean print-production and finishing area; and a dedicated 3D-printing area.
  • Fabrication: enclosed space for laser cutters, a waterjet, CNC routers and mills, metal bending, assembly and finishing. Separate noise, dust, fumes and production traffic from customers and food.
  • Support space: sheet and stock storage, paper and consumables, loading and receiving, finished orders, toilets and staff facilities.
  • Our working allocation is 1,500 sq ft for fabrication, marketing printing and 3D printing combined, with materials storage allocated separately. Confirm the selected machines and their operating and service clearances against a floor plan.
  • Assess electrical service, floor loading, delivery-door sizes, ventilation and dust extraction, compressed air, and water and drainage requirements for the chosen equipment.
  • Confirm permission for the full mix of café, retail, classes, events and fabrication. A commercial or flex-industrial property with an approved public-facing area may be a better fit than a conventional storefront.
  • In Post Falls, Community Commercial Services is a starting point to investigate for restaurant, retail and education uses; the actual fabrication processes need a separate use determination.
  • Obtain the applicable building, fire and food-service approvals. A café requires health review; cooking and fabrication equipment may require dedicated ventilation or fire protection.

Fire protection: check the building, not just its listing

Sprinkler requirements depend on the occupancy, fire area, occupant load, floor level and local amendments. The 2018 model code includes Group E school triggers such as a fire area over 12,000 square feet and certain spaces below the exit-discharge level. Mixed café, event and workshop uses can trigger different requirements. A small school is not automatically exempt. Obtain a property-specific determination from the building and fire officials.

Bring to the first conversation

  • Address, floor plan, room dimensions and the intended use: school, Tink Town or both.
  • Existing zoning, certificate of occupancy, sprinkler and alarm information, and any prior approvals.
  • Availability, rent expectations, parking, utilities and willingness to make necessary improvements.

Agree before starting

  • Written confirmation of permitted uses and the route to building, fire and health approvals.
  • A viable classroom layout or café/workshop plan, including costs of any conversion.
  • Rent, lease length, responsibility for improvements and the conditions that must be met before occupancy.

02 / Provide the foundation

Equip the work.

Help equip a place where a sketch can become a part, an idea can become a prototype, and a business can get useful work done.

How this could work

We are building toward a complete path from idea to finished work: design it, fabricate it, print it and put it to use. Start with software, creative work and modest print equipment; add 3D printing and fabrication as demand supports them. The list below describes the wider capability we can grow toward, not a day-one shopping list.

Digital fabrication

SendCutSend shows the kind of capability we want to bring within reach locally. Our equipment plan includes:

  • Fiber laser cutters for precision metal parts, signs, brackets and panels.
  • CO₂ laser cutters for suitable materials such as acrylic and wood.
  • Abrasive waterjet cutters for composites and other materials that need a different cutting process.
  • CNC routers for wood, plastics and composite panels; CNC mills for three-dimensional parts machined from solid stock.
  • CNC press brakes for bending sheet metal, plus deburring, tapping, countersinking, hardware insertion and finishing tools.
  • Documented SendCutSend examples include AMADA ENSIS 3015 AJ and VENTIS 3015 AJ fiber lasers, an AMADA EGB 6020 press brake and OMAX waterjet equipment. These are reference examples, not a final purchasing list.

Marketing print production

The everyday usefulness of a print shop: help a business design its message, then produce the materials that carry it.

  • A production color printer or digital press for flyers, brochures, business cards, menus and booklets.
  • Wide-format printing for posters, banners, signs and displays; vinyl cutting for lettering and decals.
  • Trimming, folding, creasing, binding, laminating and mounting equipment for a professional finished result.
  • Design workstations, color-management tools, paper and media storage, and clean space for finishing and packing.

3D printing and prototyping

Filament and resin 3D printers for prototypes, models, replacement parts and custom products, matched to the material and performance each job needs. Include washing and curing equipment where needed, material storage, measuring tools and finishing benches.

AI, robotics and creative work

Computers and software for design, automation and AI-assisted work; robotics and electronics equipment for projects; cameras, sound, lighting and editing tools for marketing content, courses and events.

Bring to the first conversation

  • Equipment you own or would consider funding, including model, condition and specifications.
  • What it can make or enable, purchase cost and availability.
  • Power, ventilation, space, maintenance and operator-training requirements.

Agree before starting

  • Ownership, lease payments, duration and any purchase or return option.
  • Installation, safe operation, training and consumables.
  • Maintenance, insurance, damage and any proposed use by participating schools.

03 / Provide the foundation

Finance the next step.

Help finance a defined stage of development through a business loan. The idea is a clear interest arrangement and a visible local purpose, without requiring you to buy ownership.

How this could work

Our working proposal is 6% annual interest, subject to a credible repayment plan, agreed terms and appropriate review. The goal is interest income alongside local impact, rather than stock-market-style ownership upside.

Working proposal: 6% a year

For illustration, $10,000 outstanding for a full year at 6% simple annual interest would accrue $600 before tax. This illustrates the proposed rate; it does not set payment frequency, fees, compounding or a commitment to lend or borrow.

A business loan, not a savings account

Savings rates vary, so we do not promise to match or beat them. A loan to our business would not have bank-deposit insurance. Repayment depends on the business, and some or all of the capital could be lost.

Bring to the first conversation

  • The approximate amount and lending period you would consider.
  • Your preferred payment timing and whether you would need access to the money early.
  • The information you would need to assess the purpose, repayment plan and risks.

Agree before starting

  • The borrower, permitted use of funds and conditions before release.
  • The final interest rate, interest calculation, payment schedule, maturity and early repayment terms.
  • Any security or guarantees, reporting, missed-payment provisions and the applicable legal requirements.
Your first step

Discuss the proposed rate and purpose with us. No money should change hands until the repayment plan, risks and final loan documents are agreed.

04 / Put talent to work

Bring it in. Let’s make it happen.

A part you need made. A business that needs a fresh look. Hours lost to repetitive work. Bring us something concrete to improve.

How this could work

We start with a design, a sketch, a sample or a walkthrough of how your business works. Then we identify a useful first result, quote the work we can deliver and agree how to judge it. These services form our intended starting offer as Tink Town develops.

Design it. Print it. Make it.

Bring your own 3D model, a broken part, a sketch or an idea. We can explore design assistance, prototypes, custom fixtures, display pieces and replacement parts. Materials, tolerances and suitability must be checked; cutting or machining may be sourced from a specialist.

Bring your business’s image forward

Videos, websites, advertising creative, product photography and a refreshed visual identity. The aim is to help customers understand your business and see the quality of what you do.

Find the work that should not take so long

Review intake, quoting, scheduling, customer follow-up, data entry and document handling. Identify waste, test AI or automation where it fits, and use robotics where physical tasks justify it. Compare time, cost and quality before and after; keep human review where judgment matters.

Bring to the first conversation

  • A file, sketch, sample, existing website or example of a task that wastes time.
  • The result you need, quantities or scope, deadline and budget range.
  • Material or quality requirements, and any systems the work would need to connect to.

Agree before starting

  • A specific deliverable or pilot, with price, timing and acceptance criteria.
  • Materials, manufacturing limits, revisions and ownership of the resulting work.
  • Permission to use necessary information, privacy and human checks for automated work.
Your first step

05 / Put talent to work

Hire emerging talent.

Find people who can think, communicate, build and use emerging technology. We are exploring an employer-paid placement service alongside our education and project work.

How this could work

A business describes a real role. With the candidate’s permission, we identify a potential fit using skills, work samples and relevant experience. The employer interviews and makes the hiring decision.

A placement fee, paid by the employer

Our proposed model is a fixed success fee agreed before an introduction and payable when a qualifying hire starts. The candidate would not pay the placement fee. Fee amounts, qualifying introductions and any replacement or refund terms remain to be defined.

See what people can actually do

Projects can provide evidence of practical ability: a working prototype, an automation, a video, a website or a record of reliable teamwork. School participation alone is not a guarantee of readiness for a particular job.

Bring to the first conversation

  • The role, work involved, skills, location and expected start date.
  • Hours, compensation range and supervision or training available.
  • Your hiring process and the evidence you need to evaluate a candidate.

Agree before starting

  • The employer’s written placement terms, fee trigger and any replacement or refund arrangement.
  • Candidate consent and appropriate handling of applications and work samples.
  • Employment conditions and any age, safety or supervision requirements.
Your first step

06 / Build capability together

Multiply what your team can do.

Teach your people to use AI and automation on the work they actually do. Less time copying, chasing and formatting; more time thinking, serving customers and moving the business forward.

How this could work

Begin with a short workflow review, choose a repetitive task, measure the current effort, then teach and implement a better way. The goal is a working improvement, not a presentation everyone forgets. Tenfold gains are an ambition for suitable tasks, not a promised result for every employee or business.

AI for the everyday administrator

Turn meeting notes into actions, draft replies for review, summarize documents, prepare reports and organize repeatable office work. Staff leave with workflows they can use, plus rules for checking accuracy and protecting information.

Agentic Mindset

A proposed core course: break work into clear tasks, decide what to automate or delegate to an AI agent, set boundaries, check the result and improve the process. Learn to direct technology with judgment rather than simply ask it questions.

A business improvement sprint

Map intake, quoting, scheduling or customer follow-up. Identify a bottleneck, build a small pilot and train the people who will use it. Measure time saved, errors, quality and the amount of human oversight required.

Bring to the first conversation

  • One task or workflow that is slow, repetitive or difficult to manage.
  • Who performs it, their experience and the tools they already use.
  • Group size, preferred format, timing, budget and the result you want to measure.

Agree before starting

  • The chosen workflow, learning outcomes and any implementation deliverables.
  • The venue, instructor, approved tools and rules for company or customer data.
  • Price, follow-up support and how the improvement will be measured.
Your first step

Tell us what your administrator or team spends too much time doing. We will explore a workshop or pilot built around that task.

07 / Build capability together

Bring your team into what’s next.

Give your people an evening worth talking about: good food, emerging technology, useful ideas and a practical challenge to take back to work.

How this could work

We are developing a recurring Tink Town gathering for local businesses: part dinner, part keynote and demonstration, part team exercise. The aim is to make learning about the future a normal, enjoyable part of business life.

Proposed series: Agentic Mindset evenings

A monthly or quarterly format to explore: dinner and conversation, a focused keynote with a live demonstration, then a guided group exercise. Each team leaves with one practical experiment to try before the next session.

A place to be part of the future

Meet other local builders, try emerging tools and see what businesses are making possible. Your company can host clients, bring its staff, sponsor an evening or co-create a workshop with us.

From interesting evening to useful habit

Possible themes include AI for office work, better customer follow-up, design-to-prototype, and deciding when an agent or robot is the right tool. Recurring sessions can revisit what teams tried and what actually improved.

Bring to the first conversation

  • Your team or audience, likely group size and the theme that would matter to them.
  • Whether you want a private company session, a public event or recurring participation.
  • Timing, budget and anything you could contribute, such as a speaker, demonstration or catering.

Agree before starting

  • The date, venue, food, agenda and learning or relationship goal.
  • Hosting responsibilities, ticketing or sponsorship price, promotion and attendee information.
  • Equipment, accessibility, safety, cancellation terms and any follow-up session.
Your first step
